# Level B1: Threshold

**Prerequisite:** Milestone A2 reached (step A2-330 ticked). The README methods still apply, and the A2 weekly patterns (the podcast pattern, the AI voice sessions, the monologues, the journal, the series episode) continue with the changes below.

**Goal at exit:** you can follow the main points of clear standard French on familiar topics (the radio news, a family sitcom, a science documentary for children, a conversation about work or opinions), handle almost any everyday situation including the *préfecture*, the bank and the doctor, tell a story or explain an opinion in connected speech with reasons, read a children's classic, a short contemporary novel, a Maupassant story, a Maigret and official information, and write about 200 words of connected text in the right register (a formal letter to the *mairie*, an opinion piece, a personal narrative), with the accents and the agreements right. Vocabulary about 4000 words. You know the complete grammar of French, including the subjunctive in its common uses, the three types of *si*-clause, the *conditionnel passé* and the *futur antérieur*, the agreement of the past participle, the full pronoun order, reported speech, the passive and its substitutes, the verb + preposition combinations and the connector system; you recognise the *passé simple* on the page; and your pronunciation has the liaisons, the *enchaînement* and the rhythm of connected speech. This is the level of the DELF B1 and of the civic knowledge in the *Livret du citoyen*.

**Time:** the dailies take about 2 hours a day (half of it the commute slot and free reading, which need no desk) and the steps add up to about 440 hours: about 860 hours in all. At the planned pace, over 30 weeks, that is about 2 hours 25 minutes of steps a day, 6 days a week; at one hour of steps a day the level takes about 73 weeks. Keep the order either way.

**What changes at B1** (Appendix A): the culture track is now in French. *Fais pas ci, fais pas ça* is the television spine, one episode a week with French subtitles, and *Un gars, une fille* and *C'est pas sorcier* sit beside it every week. You read your first unabridged novels and stories by Goscinny, Schmitt, Guène, Pagnol, Daudet, Maupassant, Anouilh, Simenon and Camus, plus *Tintin*, *Astérix* and *Persepolis*, and the *Livret du citoyen*, the booklet France gives to candidates for naturalisation, becomes your non-fiction reader. *Édito B1* (Didier) is the coursebook spine: twelve units at one every two to three weeks, four sittings each, whose authentic documents, grammar and vocabulary pages, cahier and DELF B1 task do the work the TV5Monde exercises did at A2. Grammar is a systematic pass through *French Grammar and Usage* (Hawkins and Towell) from the first chapter to the last, with the *Grammaire progressive, niveau intermédiaire* and the *Bescherelle* beside it, and the weekly dictée begins. The commute slot becomes RFI's *Journal en français facile* and the audiobook of the current book. *InnerFrench*, *Français Authentique* and, from Week 11, *One Thing in a French Day* are weekly podcast steps; *Transfert* and *Les Pieds sur terre* arrive as stretches at the end. Films go from six to eleven, all with French subtitles. A poem by heart every two weeks joins the chanson.

## The B1 day

Every study day: **the dailies, in the order of the table, then the current step**, the first unticked step in this file. One step is one sitting with one focus; a step too long for today is resumed tomorrow. What used to be a weekly rhythm (the podcasts, the monologue, the bank text, the song) and the weekly habits (the series, the comprehensible-input video) are now individual steps placed in order inside each week, so there is nothing to schedule and nothing running in the background except the dailies.

| Daily | Minutes | Starts at | Ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anki: all due cards, then 20 new (a third of them sentence cards) | 20 | A1-004 | never |
| Assimil second wave, one lesson | 10 | A2-011 | when the second wave reaches lesson 100, at the end of A2 or in the first weeks of B1 (B1-157), if it is still running |
| Commute slot: the *Journal en français facile*, then the audiobook chapter of the current book already read | 30 | B1-009 | B2 changes its content |
| AI voice conversation, topic fixed by the rule in B1-011 | 15 | A2-009 | never |
| The daily song (or the current poem), sung or recited twice | 5 | A1-040 | never |
| The journal, 8 to 10 sentences, corrected on Sundays | 10 | A1-077 | end of B2 |
| Free reading, from the fixed list | 20 | A2-122 | never |

The comprehensible-input video stopped being a daily at the end of A2 (A2-317); *Easy French* and the *InnerFrench* videos are good free-time viewing, but the series, the podcasts and the commute slot now carry the listening.

A week check passes when every step since the previous check is ticked and the dailies were done on at least six of the last seven days.

The steps that return every week are written as one line and use these patterns:

- **Édito pattern** (ED, Weeks 1–28): a unit of *Édito B1* is four steps, one sitting each. (a) *Documents*: every listening and video document of the unit with the LRS cycle (Method 1; the transcripts are at the back of the livre), and every reading document intensively (unknown words looked up and written in the notebook with their sentence, the comprehension questions answered in writing and checked). (b) *Grammar and lexique*: the unit's grammar and vocabulary pages; copy each rule into the notebook with the book's example and one of your own; do every exercise of the livre in writing and check it against the key (the *guide pédagogique*; where you have no key, the Correction protocol with the exercise's instruction pasted in); rewrite every wrong answer. (c) *Cahier and cards*: the cahier d'activités pages for the unit, in writing, checked against the *corrigés*, every wrong answer rewritten; then the unit's words to Anki by the card rules (at most 30, sentence cards where the book gives a sentence). (d) *DELF task*: the unit's DELF B1 *production écrite* (about 160 words, under the exam's time where one is given) through BonPatron and the Correction protocol, filed in `writing/portfolio-B1.md`, and its *production orale* (the monologue or the role-play the unit sets) with the Recording protocol. Done when (d)'s corrections are in the notebook and the recording is saved. The steps name the units by number in the current edition; if yours has a different number of units, keep the pace of one unit every two to three weeks and merge or double the shortest so that the book ends in Week 28.
- **GU pattern**: read the chapter (or the sections) of *French Grammar and Usage* named in the step; copy every rule you did not already know into the notebook with the book's example and one of your own; do the matching chapter of *Practising French Grammar* and check it against the key; do the *Grammaire progressive, niveau intermédiaire* chapter or chapters on the same topic (find them in its contents) and check them against the *corrigés*, redoing any exercise under 80 percent; then write the number of sentences the step states, run the Correction protocol and copy the corrections by hand. Chapter numbers are those of the 4th edition; if yours differs, go by the chapter titles.
- **Verb-table drill** (weekly): for the three verbs named in the step, write from memory, without the book, the *présent*, *passé composé*, *imparfait*, *futur simple*, *conditionnel présent*, *subjonctif présent* and *impératif*, and from Week 9 the third-person *passé simple*; check every form against the *Bescherelle*; copy any wrong tense out in full; then say the two tenses you got wrong most through the six persons aloud, twice. Log the error count in the margin.
- **RFI pattern**: on francaisfacile.rfi.fr, under the B1 exercises, the next exercise in the site's order: listen twice, do the exercise, check, read the transcript, add 5 cards.
- **Podcast pattern** (INNER, OTFD, and the first 5 minutes of TRANS): listen blind twice, read the transcript, listen with the text, write a 5-sentence French summary and score it against the transcript, shadow 60 seconds, add 8 cards. Episodes are taken in order from the first one.
- **FA pattern**: one *Français Authentique* episode, extensive, no transcript on the first listen; then the transcript, and three expressions copied with the sentence they came in; 5 cards.
- **Series pattern** (FPC): watch the episode once with French subtitles, no pausing. Choose one 8–10 minute scene and rewatch it three times: with subtitles, without, then pausing to shadow ten lines. Write six sentences in French about what happened and three spoken-register expressions you heard with their careful-French equivalent. Add 10 cards. In the tracker note the episode number and one thing about French family life it showed you.
- **UGUF**: three episodes of *Un gars, une fille*, no subtitles, extensive; three expressions noted. Québec option: the original *Un gars, une fille* (Radio-Canada, 1997–2003), which the French version adapted, episode for episode.
- **CPS pattern**: one *C'est pas sorcier* episode (26 minutes) on the topic named or, if none is named, the next one in the playlist you chose, once without pausing, auto-captions on if YouTube offers them; write five facts in French and the three words you would need to explain the topic; 5 cards.
- **Book pattern**: *before* each book, read the back cover and the lead of its French Wikipedia article (gist), list the characters, and where an audiobook exists listen to each chapter in the commute slot before you read it; *during*, intensive on the first quarter (every unknown word looked up, at most 15 written per chapter, 20–30 cards a week) then extensive with at most 10 lookups per sitting, and mark five sentences you like; *after* each book, an extensive reread of the best chapter, a 3-minute oral retelling (Recording protocol), an essay of 150–250 words (Correction protocol, portfolio), five imitated sentences (copy the five sentences you marked and write one of your own on each model), and the tracker row.
- **BD pattern**: read the album in one sitting without a dictionary; second pass on the first twenty pages with 15 lookups; note the onomatopoeia and the spoken-register bubbles; 10 cards; tracker.
- **Livret pattern**: read the next three or four pages of the *Livret du citoyen* intensively (every unknown word looked up, 5 cards); after each section a 4-sentence French summary in the notebook; then write three quiz questions of your own with their answers in `writing/livret-questions.md`. They are what you revise for the exit quiz.
- **Monologue**: Recording protocol on the topic named, from Appendix F part 2, 3–4 minutes, transcribed and corrected. Today's and tomorrow's voice sessions are on the topic.
- **Dictée**: the source named in the step; play it in chunks of five to eight seconds and write; reread once for agreements and accents before checking; check against the text; count every wrong or missing letter, accent, hyphen, agreement or word as one error; log the word count and the errors per 100 words in `writing/dictees.md`; copy each wrong word correctly three times.
- **Song**: write out the last song from memory and check it; then the new song from the Appendix J part 1 list, in order: listen twice with the lyrics, look up every word, sing along three times. It is the daily song until the next song or poem step.
- **Poem**: read the poem with a native reading (Litterature audio or YouTube), look up every word, copy it by hand, learn it four lines a day; it is recited in the daily-song slot until the next song or poem step; on the day of the next poem step, recite the old one from memory, recorded.

- [ ] **B1-017** [Study · Deep · 60 min] Liaison and *enchaînement*, part 1.
      Do: French runs words together inside a *groupe rythmique* and stresses only its last syllable. Rules: (1) *enchaînement*: a pronounced final consonant joins the next word's vowel (*il est* → *i-lest*, *avec elle* → *a-ve-kelle*, *une amie* → *u-na-mie*); (2) obligatory liaison: determiner + noun (*les amis* with *z*, *un homme* with *n*, *mon ami*), pronoun + verb (*nous avons, ils ont, vous êtes*), adjective + noun before a vowel (*petit enfant* with *t*, *grand homme* with *t*, *bon ami* with *n* and a denasalised vowel), after one-syllable prepositions and adverbs (*en avril, chez eux, très utile, dans un*), *est* and *c'est* in careful speech (*c'est un, il est ici*); (3) forbidden liaison: after *et*, before *h aspiré* (*les | haricots, les | héros*), after a singular noun (*un étudiant | anglais*), before *oui, onze, un* as a number; (4) the sounds: *s* and *x* liaise as *z*, *d* as *t*, *f* in *neuf heures* as *v*; (5) optional liaisons (after plural nouns, after *pas*, *est* in casual speech) mark register: more liaisons, more formal. ## Appendix A: What changes at B1

1. **French about French.** Grammar help now comes first from GU in English with the *Grammaire progressive* and the *Bescherelle* beside it, and from Larousse's *difficultés* pages and BonPatron in French. Culture reading is in French (French Wikipedia, vie-publique.fr, Lumni, Wikisource, Litterature audio). If a page is too hard, use text-to-speech and read for gist; do not switch to English.
2. **Native speed is the norm.** The *Journal en français facile* is slow news, but *Fais pas ci, fais pas ça*, *Un gars, une fille*, *C'est pas sorcier*, *One Thing in a French Day*, *Transfert* and *Les Pieds sur terre* are made for French speakers. Extensive listening with no pausing is most of your listening time now; the podcast pattern (with transcripts) keeps the intensive side alive.
3. **A family sitcom as spine.** Twenty-eight episodes of *Fais pas ci, fais pas ça*, one a week. It is the series French families of the 2010s watched together, its dialogue is the everyday French of parents, teenagers and schools, and every episode is a lesson in the spoken register (*on*, dropped *ne*, *du coup*, *t'as vu*) that the grammar books describe and the dictée forbids. The scene-shadowing part of the pattern is where the pronunciation gain is.
4. **Books, not readers.** Goscinny, Schmitt, Guène, Daudet, Pagnol, Maupassant, Anouilh, Simenon and Camus, plus three BD series. The book pattern's intensive-then-extensive rule matters more the longer the book: with Pagnol you go extensive after 45 pages. The *passé simple* is learned for recognition in Week 9 because Daudet, Pagnol, Maupassant and Simenon use it on every page.
5. **The dictée begins.** One a week from Week 1, graded by a strict error count, because French spelling is where agreement, accents and the sound system meet, and because the exit tests count every accent. The target goes from 15 errors per 100 words to 4.
6. **Non-fiction is official.** The *Livret du citoyen* is written in plain French about the things French people assume everyone knows: the motto, *laïcité*, the Revolution, the Fifth Republic, the regions. Read all of it, continue with the vie-publique.fr *fiches*, and answer the quiz at the exit.
7. **The steps that return every week** hold the A2 habits at higher volume: one or two Édito sittings, an RFI exercise in most weeks, three podcasts, the series, the sitcom, the science programme, a monologue, a dictée, a bank text, a verb drill, a grammar chapter, two or three book sittings, a song or a poem. They are not optional; with the dailies they are most of the hours.
8. **Grammar goes systematic.** Every chapter of GU in order, with the GPI chapters and the *Practising French Grammar* exercises, and your own sentences as the test; the Édito units present the same points in French, in the book's own order, so most rules are met twice. The subjunctive gets two full weeks and the past participle two passes, because they are the difference between B1 and everything below. By Week 28 nothing in the grammar should be new; from then on grammar work is only repair.
9. **Pronunciation moves to connected speech.** Liaison, *enchaînement*, the mute *e*, the final-syllable stress and the rising groups are what make you sound French rather than understandable. The two liaison steps and the scene shadowing carry this.
10. **The DELF B1 is the model exam.** Every Édito unit ends with a DELF B1 task, so its four papers are familiar long before the end; their formats shape the last three weeks; the official *exemple de sujet* is the exit check, and the civic quiz sits beside it.
11. **Time.** The level is long. Count weeks completed, not weeks elapsed: a missed week is added at the end, not skipped.

## Appendix B: B1 grammar checklist

1. **Nouns**: gender by ending and the exception list, double-gender nouns, person nouns and their feminines, plurals in *-x* and *-aux*, compound plurals, *gens*, collectives.
2. **Determiners**: the definite article with generic, abstract and body-part nouns and with days; omission with professions; the partitive and its reduction to *de* after negation and quantities; *des* to *de* before a plural adjective; *ce/cet/cette/ces*; *mon* before a feminine vowel; *tout, chaque, quelques, plusieurs, certains, aucun, n'importe quel*.
3. **Adjectives**: feminine and plural forms, invariable colours, position and the meaning-changing adjectives (*ancien, grand, propre, pauvre, certain, dernier, cher, seul, simple*), comparison with *meilleur / pire / moindre*, the superlative with *de*, *de plus en plus*, *plus ... plus*.
4. **Tenses**: present, *passé composé*, *imparfait*, *plus-que-parfait*, *futur simple*, *futur antérieur*, *conditionnel présent* and *passé*, *venir de*, *être en train de*, *aller* + infinitive; *passé composé* against *imparfait* in narrative; the verbs whose meaning shifts with the tense (*savoir, vouloir, pouvoir, devoir, avoir*); *depuis, il y a ... que, ça fait ... que*.
5. ***Passé simple* recognised**: the third-person forms of the main verbs and of *être, avoir, faire, venir, tenir, aller, dire, prendre, mettre, voir, vivre, savoir, pouvoir, devoir, vouloir, écrire, naître, mourir, recevoir, croire, connaître, lire, courir, ouvrir, s'asseoir*; the *passé antérieur*.
6. **Subjunctive, forms**: the regular pattern, the two-stem verbs, the ten irregulars (*être, avoir, aller, faire, pouvoir, savoir, vouloir, falloir, valoir, pleuvoir*), the past subjunctive.
7. **Subjunctive, triggers**: will and necessity, emotion, doubt and denial, judgement; the indicative after belief and certainty in the affirmative and after *espérer*; the infinitive with one subject.
8. **Subjunctive, conjunctions and relatives**: *pour que, afin que, avant que, jusqu'à ce que, bien que, quoique, à condition que, pourvu que, à moins que, sans que, de peur que*; the indicative after *après que, parce que, puisque, pendant que, dès que, depuis que, tandis que, alors que*; superlative and *le seul* antecedents, wanted and negated antecedents; *qui que, quoi que, où que, quel que*.
9. ***Si*-clauses and hypothesis**: the three types and the mixed type, never the future or the conditional after *si*, *si* = whether, *au cas où* + conditional, *comme si* + *imparfait*, *même si*, *à condition que / de*.
10. **Irregular verbs**: the A2 list plus *acquérir, apercevoir, atteindre, battre, bouillir, conduire, construire, craindre, cueillir, décevoir, éteindre, fuir, haïr, interdire, joindre, mourir, naître, peindre, plaindre, plaire, produire, résoudre, rire, satisfaire, se taire, suivre, traduire, vaincre, vivre*, and the spelling-change verbs (*manger, commencer, payer, essuyer, appeler, jeter, acheter, lever, espérer, préférer*), checked in the *Bescherelle*.
11. **Object pronouns in full**: the order *me/te/se/nous/vous < le/la/les < lui/leur < y < en*, the affirmative imperative order with *-moi* and *-m'en*, *y* and *en* and their limits, the neutral *le*, the stressed pronouns, *on*.
12. **Past participle agreement**: with *être*, with a preceding direct object, not with *en* or an indirect object, pronominal verbs by the function of *se*, *fait* + infinitive invariable, the impersonal and measure cases.
13. **The passive and its substitutes**: *être* + participle in every tense, *par* and *de*, direct objects only, *on*, the pronominal passive, *se faire* + infinitive, *faire* + infinitive, *laisser* + infinitive.
14. **Reported speech**: the tense shifts after a past reporting verb, imperative to *de* + infinitive, the time and place words, indirect questions with *si, ce que, ce qui*, no inversion.

## Appendix E: B1 exit reading test

**Du bio à la cantine : à quel prix ?**

Depuis la rentrée, le collège Jules-Ferry de notre commune sert deux repas bio par semaine à ses six cents élèves. La décision a été prise au printemps par le conseil départemental, qui gère les cantines des collèges, après une pétition de parents d'élèves. Le menu du mardi et celui du jeudi sont désormais composés de produits issus de l'agriculture biologique, en grande partie achetés à des fermes situées à moins de cinquante kilomètres.

En tant que mère de deux collégiens, je me réjouis de cette décision. Mes enfants mangent mieux, et ils l'ont remarqué eux-mêmes : le pain a du goût, les légumes ne sont plus bouillis dans l'eau, et le poisson du jeudi vient du port voisin au lieu d'arriver congelé de l'autre bout du monde. Le chef de la cantine, qui travaillait auparavant dans un restaurant, a réorganisé sa cuisine pour préparer les plats sur place.

Mais la mesure a un coût. Le prix du repas est passé de 3,20 euros à 3,80 euros, soit une hausse de près de vingt pour cent. Pour une famille de trois enfants, cela représente plus de deux cents euros de plus par an. Le département a certes élargi les tarifs réduits aux familles les plus modestes, mais beaucoup de parents se situent juste au-dessus du seuil et paient le tarif plein. Plusieurs d'entre eux ont annoncé qu'ils donneraient désormais un sandwich à leurs enfants, ce qui serait exactement le contraire du but recherché.

Il existe pourtant des solutions. Dans la commune voisine, le collège a réduit le gaspillage de moitié en proposant deux tailles de portions et en pesant chaque semaine ce qui part à la poubelle ; l'argent économisé a permis de passer au bio sans augmenter les prix. D'autres établissements ont remplacé la viande par un plat végétarien un jour par semaine, ce qui coûte moins cher et que la loi demande de toute façon depuis 2019.

Je ne demande donc pas qu'on revienne en arrière. Je demande que le conseil départemental fasse ce que d'autres ont déjà fait : commencer par le gaspillage, et faire en sorte que le bio ne devienne pas un luxe réservé aux familles qui peuvent se le permettre. Une cantine, c'est d'abord le lieu où tous les enfants mangent la même chose à la même table. Ce serait dommage que la meilleure nourriture serve à les séparer.

*Nadia Benali, Saint-Brieuc*

## Appendix I: Writing task bank

About 200 words each unless stated. Closed book. BonPatron, then the Correction protocol. Clean copies with error counts to `writing/portfolio-B1.md`. In the second half of the level each task is done again and compared with the first version.

1. An email to a French-speaking friend about your first impressions of *Fais pas ci, fais pas ça*.
2. A message to your *syndic* or *copropriété* about a lift that has been broken for two weeks (formal, with a requested deadline).
3. A short *lettre de motivation* for a job you could plausibly get: why you, three skills, availability.
4. A CV in French (structure, not prose; one page; *expérience professionnelle, formation, compétences, langues, centres d'intérêt*).
5. A retelling of an FPC episode for someone who has not seen it (past tenses, 200 words).
6. A film review of *Les Quatre Cents Coups* or *La Haine*: the plot in four sentences, two things you liked, one you did not, who should watch it.
7. A story that begins « Quand je suis rentré(e) ce soir-là, la porte était ouverte. »
8. A description of your hometown for a French-speaking audience, including one thing they would find strange.
9. A formal letter to your *mairie* about a problem in your street (traffic, rubbish, lighting), asking for a specific action by a specific date, with the full closing formula.
10. An email to your child's teacher, your own teacher or your manager asking for time off or an exception, with a reason and a proposal.
11. A recipe from your home country with a short introduction on when it is eaten (infinitives or the imperative for the steps).
12. A book review of *Le Petit Nicolas* or *Kiffe kiffe demain*.
13. An opinion text (*courrier des lecteurs*) on « Faut-il interdire les téléphones portables au collège ? »: introduction, two arguments, one counter-argument answered, conclusion.
14. An opinion text on « Faut-il créer plus de zones sans voitures dans les villes ? » in the same shape.
15. A letter to a friend abroad explaining a French tradition (le réveillon, la galette des Rois, le 14 juillet, la Fête de la musique, le 1er mai et le muguet, la Chandeleur) and what you think of it.
16. A text in the DELF B1 style (about 160 words): an experience that changed your opinion about something, for a magazine's readers' page.
